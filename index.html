Understanding
OCD.

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder is more than just liking things clean or organized. It is a mental health condition that can affect how a person thinks, feels, and lives every day. Learning about OCD is the first step toward understanding it.

01 — The basics

What is OCD?

OCD stands for Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder. It is a mental health condition that can cause unwanted thoughts and repeated actions that are difficult to control.

01 / OBSESSIONS

Unwanted thoughts

Obsessions are unwanted thoughts, fears, or images that keep coming back. For example, a person may constantly worry about germs, danger, or making a mistake.

02 / COMPULSIONS

Repeated actions

Compulsions are repeated actions or mental routines a person feels they must do. For example, they may wash their hands many times or repeatedly check a locked door.

OCD is not just a habit.

These thoughts and actions can take a lot of time, cause stress, and make daily life harder. OCD is not a person's fault, and effective treatment is available.

02 — Signs to know

Common symptoms

OCD affects people in different ways. These are some examples of what a person with OCD may experience.

↻

Repeated checking

Checking doors, appliances, or other things again and again because of fear that something bad might happen.

✳

Fear of germs

Feeling very worried about dirt, germs, or getting sick and feeling a strong need to clean or wash.

◎

Unwanted thoughts

Having upsetting thoughts that keep returning, even when the person does not want to think about them.

≋

Need for things to feel right

Feeling strong discomfort when things seem uneven, out of order, or not done in a certain way.

Remember:

Having one of these experiences does not automatically mean someone has OCD. A qualified health professional can help with diagnosis.

03 — Understanding why

What causes OCD?

There is no single known cause of OCD. Researchers believe several things may play a role.

BIOLOGY

Brain and genetics

Brain processes and family history may affect a person's chance of developing OCD. Scientists are still studying the exact causes.

LIFE EXPERIENCES

Stress and life events

Stressful experiences may make OCD symptoms start or become worse. However, stress alone does not explain every case of OCD.

04 — Finding help

OCD can be treated.

OCD can be difficult, but people can learn ways to manage their symptoms and improve their lives.

Therapy

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) can help people change how they respond to unwanted thoughts and fears.

ERP treatment

Exposure and Response Prevention helps people face fears step by step without performing their usual compulsions.

Medication

A health professional may recommend medicine to help reduce OCD symptoms. Treatment depends on each person's needs.

Asking for help is okay.

If OCD is affecting your daily life, consider talking to a doctor or mental health professional. You do not have to handle everything alone.
